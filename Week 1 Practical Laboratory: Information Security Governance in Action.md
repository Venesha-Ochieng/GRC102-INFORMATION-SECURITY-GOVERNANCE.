## Laboratory Title

### Information Security Governance in Action

This report presents a practical analysis of information security governance within the fictional organization *GlobalHealth Connect (GHC)*. The assessment focuses on governance structures, security leadership responsibilities, board-level security reporting, risk oversight mechanisms, steering committee operations, and security governance maturity improvement strategies.

---

## Executive Summary

GlobalHealth Connect (GHC) has experienced rapid growth through two strategic acquisitions and now operates a cloud-based patient management platform without a formal information security governance function. Following a contained data leakage near-miss, the Board commissioned a comprehensive governance transformation initiative to strengthen oversight, accountability, and risk management across the organization.

This report addresses that mandate through five interconnected workstreams:

1. Development of a governance blueprint that establishes clear organizational structure, accountability, and decision-making authority.
2. Creation of a formal Information Security Charter to define the security programme's purpose, scope, and governance principles.
3. Analysis of Board-level security metrics to support strategic oversight and informed decision-making.
4. Establishment of a Security Steering Committee (SSC) to resolve cross-functional security issues, demonstrated through a password policy dispute involving the Chief Technology Officer (CTO) and IT Manager.
5. Assessment of governance maturity and development of a 12–18 month roadmap to achieve Level 3 (Defined) maturity across six critical governance domains.

# Task 1: Governance Blueprint

## 1.1 Executive Requirement

Following a recent data-leakage near-miss, the Chief Executive Officer has requested the design of a formal Information Security Governance structure that establishes:

- Clear security leadership and accountability.
- Defined reporting relationships.
- Effective Board oversight.
- Risk-based decision-making processes.
- Alignment between business objectives and security requirements.

The governance model must support GHC's continued growth while ensuring the protection of patient data, regulatory compliance, and operational resilience.

---

## 1.2 Current Organizational Context

The existing structure demonstrates several governance gaps, including informal security responsibilities, limited oversight mechanisms, and unclear accountability for enterprise security risks.

| Employee / Role | Department | Relevant Context |
|---------------|------------|------------------|
| John Smith – IT Manager | IT Operations | Manages infrastructure and performs security activities without formal governance authority. |
| Jane Doe – Senior Developer | Development | Leads a critical development team responsible for application delivery. |
| Mark Johnson – Compliance Officer | Legal & Compliance | Responsible for healthcare regulatory compliance and audit coordination. |
| Emily White – Data Analyst | Finance | Processes and accesses sensitive financial information. |
| Robert Green – HR Manager | Human Resources | Manages onboarding, offboarding, and workforce-related controls. |

### Key Governance Issues Identified

1. No dedicated Information Security Governance function.
2. Security activities are concentrated within IT Operations.
3. No enterprise-wide security decision-making forum.
4. Lack of formal reporting to executive leadership and the Board.
5. Undefined ownership of information security risks.
6. Inconsistent governance practices following organizational acquisitions.
